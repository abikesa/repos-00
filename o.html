The Scale of Uganda's Kidney Crisis

Uganda's 44 million people face a compounding renal burden — high CKD prevalence, vanishingly sparse dialysis infrastructure, and zero kidney transplants performed domestically. The numbers below are not estimates. They are a call to map the gradient.

2–7%
Community CKD Prevalence
General population studies in Uganda. Rises to 15% among HIV-positive individuals — a population disproportionately represented in Uganda.
PMC9438417 · Global Dialysis Perspective: Uganda
12.5%
Eastern Uganda CKD Rate
Over 3× the rate of southwestern Uganda (3.9%). Geographic variation this extreme demands a district-level atlas — aggregated national figures are meaningless for policy.
Muiru et al., PLOS One 2020 · SEARCH-CKD Study
135
Districts in Uganda
Fewer than 12 have any published CKD prevalence data. That is a data desert covering over 90% of Ugandan territory. This is the gap Ukubona × UCU will close.
Ukubona Atlas Landscape Survey · 2026
4
Dialysis Centers Nationally
For a country of 44 million. Mulago and Kiruddu hospitals provide ~2,600 dialysis sessions per month — the tip of an enormous iceberg of unmet need.

~0
Kidney Transplants in Uganda*
Uganda has performed fewer than 10 transplants domestically (Mulago 2023–24 pilot, now suspended). All patients requiring transplant are referred abroad — to Kenya, India, or Turkey.

A landmark rural East Africa study found that two-thirds of CKD cases have no identifiable risk factor from the standard Western checklist. This is not data noise — it is a signal that Uganda's CKD drivers include factors yet to be systematically studied: endemic infections, nephrotoxic herbal medicines, agrochemical exposures, and geographic-specific genetic variants.

Eastern Uganda shows 12.5% CKD prevalence vs 3.9% in southwestern Uganda — a 3-fold difference within a single country. This gradient is the core research signal. A district-level atlas will quantify it. A well-powered grant will explain it.

Transplant Deprivation

60 Years of Transplant
Tourism & Deprivation

Uganda has never built a sustained domestic kidney transplant program. For 60 years, patients able to afford the cost have been exported to Kenya, India, and Turkey. The rest face dialysis or palliative care — or nothing.

1960s–1990s
No kidney replacement therapy infrastructure in Uganda. Patients with ESRD receive palliative care only. Kenya develops transplant capacity at Kenyatta National Hospital.
2000s–2010s
Uganda patients begin traveling to India and Kenya for transplants. Cost: $25,000–$35,000 USD. Only 2 dialysis chairs and 2 nephrologists in Uganda as of 2012. Informal transplant tourism accelerates.
2020
Uganda passes the Human Organ Donation and Tissue Transplant Bill, awaiting Presidential assent. Law would designate transplant centers, regulate consent, and permit domestic transplants for the first time.
2022
Uganda has 94 dialysis chairs and 14 nephrologists — remarkable growth from 2012. Parliament debates transplant legislation. India and Turkey remain primary destinations; estimated cost differential: $30,000 abroad vs $8,000 domestically.
2023–2024
Mulago National Referral Hospital successfully pilots 4 kidney transplants — first domestic transplants in Ugandan history. Program suspended pending Ministry of Health regulatory framework. Patients return to medical tourism queue.
2026 →
This collaboration. Ukubona × UCU builds the first district-level atlas of CKD burden in Uganda — generating the preliminary data needed to justify NIH NIDDK / Wellcome / NPA grant funding for systematic intervention.

Optimization Framework

Learning Loop

This is not a static database project. It is an iterative optimization system — each grant cycle is one update step that moves the entire Ugandan health landscape toward the clinical ideal. The Pentad framework formalizes this.

θt

Choose the Disease Layer

CKD, ESRD, Hypertension, Diabetes — the dropdown menu that defines which parameter space we are optimizing. First iteration: CKD/ESRD across all 135 districts.

Find the Steepest Need

The ROYGBIV heat map is not decoration — it is the gradient. Districts shaded red are where ∂L/∂θ is largest: the highest burden, the biggest data gap, the most intervention leverage.

First Iteration — What UCU Gets

Deliverables for the
UCU Partnership

Prof. Basaza asked for an initial concept and timelines. Here is the complete first iteration — from proof-of-concept infrastructure to publishable datasets to student engagement frameworks.

Living Disease Atlas Dashboard
A publicly accessible web platform hosted at ukubona-llc.github.io showing district-level CKD, ESRD, hypertension, and diabetes prevalence. Dropdown-selectable disease layers. ROYGBIV loss gradient. Updated with each new literature review cycle. Embeddable in UCU's website and grant applications.

Format: Live URL + citable DOI · Timeline: Wk 10
Systematic Literature Review + Database
Comprehensive PubMed + AJOL + Grey Literature review of all published CKD, ESRD, diabetes, and hypertension studies in Uganda. Standardized extraction template. District-level geocoded dataset. Published as UCU-led data paper with student co-authors.

Format: REDCap / Excel database + pre-print · Timeline: Wk 4
Student Research Pipeline
Structured framework for UCU MPH and medical students to contribute to data extraction, quality control, and district gap analysis. Each student generates a citable contribution toward the atlas. Pipeline produces 3–5 student co-authors per publication cycle.

Format: SOPs + Author contribution log · Timeline: Wk 1
Grant Pre-Application Package
A polished NIH-format specific aims page + preliminary data summary built directly from Phase I–II outputs. Tailored versions for NIH NIDDK R01/R21, Wellcome Trust African Programmes, and NPA Uganda. Includes budget templates and UCU letter of support language.

Format: PDF + editable Word · Timeline: Month 4
CKD Risk Factor Map (NPA-Ready)
A proof-of-concept visualization specifically designed for Uganda's National Planning Authority — framing kidney disease within the NPA's NCD priority framework. Side-by-side comparison with mental health burden (NPA's stated priority) to argue for parallel investment.

Format: Interactive HTML map + 2-page brief · Timeline: Wk 6
Transplant Tourism Audit
First-of-its-kind quantitative estimate of Ugandans who have undergone kidney transplant abroad — using Ministry of Health referral records, hospital billing data from Nairobi and Indian partner hospitals, and social media signal analysis. Documents the equity and economic case for domestic capacity.

Format: Policy brief + data appendix · Timeline: Month 3
Grant Strategy

Funding Pathways for
Phase I → Scale

Multiple realistic funding vehicles exist for this collaboration. The NIH K08 pedigree and Hopkins institutional backing provide credibility that unlocks funding categories typically inaccessible to Uganda-only proposals.

NIH NIDDK — R21 Exploratory
$275,000 · 2 years
Fit: ★★★★★
The Muzaale NIH K08-AG065520 track record in living donor research is a direct match for NIDDK's Uganda renal burden portfolio. An R21 for "characterizing CKD epidemiology in sub-Saharan Africa using geospatial mapping" aligns with NIDDK PA-21-213. UCU provides the community access and local infrastructure. Hopkins provides the methods and grant management rigor.
Wellcome Trust — African Programmes
£150,000–£500,000 · 3–5 years
Fit: ★★★★☆
Wellcome's Science in Africa programme specifically funds early-career African scientists embedded in African institutions. Prof. Basaza + UCU student pipeline makes this a strong fit. The CKD-HIV nexus and unknown etiology angle aligns with Wellcome's infectious disease ↔ NCD intersection priority area.
Uganda National Planning Authority
$30,000–$80,000 · Pilot
Fit: ★★★☆☆
NPA's stated priority is mental health. The strategic approach: deliver a CKD-mental health co-morbidity brief showing that depression is twice as prevalent in CKD patients. This reframes kidney mapping as mental health infrastructure. The atlas produces data NPA cannot currently generate internally — a strong leverage position.
International Society of Nephrology — SOROCA
$10,000–$50,000 · 1 year
Fit: ★★★★☆
ISN's Special Operations for Renal Outcomes in Central Africa grant is specifically designed for low-resource CKD mapping projects in Africa. The UCU-Hopkins partnership is ideal — combines local capacity (UCU) with international methods credibility (Hopkins K08). Serves as seed funding before R21.
